Tabby Pulse — boxless glass layouts

Three different ways to organize the dashboard without using cards. All keep the glass aesthetic. Pick the one that feels right and I'll port it across the app.

A · Editorial Canvas B · Section Bands C · Floating Layers

A · Editorial Canvas

One flowing column on a single glass canvas. Hairline dividers (1px, 5% opacity) separate sections instead of cards. KPIs are typographic — large numbers separated by vertical borders, not enclosed in tiles. Tables sit flush against the canvas with subtle row hover. Feels like reading a well-typeset report.
ProsQuiet, focused, scannable. Numbers hold their own visual weight without competing chrome. Tables become first-class content.
ConsLess playful. Action affordances (buttons, hover) need to work harder to stand out.

B · Section Bands

Each section is a full-width horizontal band. Alternating bands carry a very subtle purple/green tint (~4% opacity) — enough to delineate without feeling boxy. The band's title and short description sit on the left, the actual content (KPIs, table) takes the right column. Strong sense of rhythm.
ProsSections are unambiguous. Section titles double as navigation. Tinted backgrounds remind users of glass without using card chrome.
ConsTwo-column grid eats horizontal space — content area is ~70% of width. Less suitable for very dense tables.

C · Floating Layers

Translucent glass sheets overlap with slight horizontal offsets, creating depth. Each sheet has a different blur intensity — the top sheet is sharpest, the back ones progressively more frosted. No hard edges anywhere; sections separated by depth alone. Closest to Apple Vision Pro / Apple Music aesthetic.
ProsDistinctive, premium feel. Creates a sense of layering and information hierarchy that's hard to replicate elsewhere.
ConsThe most opinionated visually — may compete with content. Harder to extend cleanly to deeply nested pages or busy lists.
