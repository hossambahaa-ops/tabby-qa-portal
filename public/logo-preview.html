Pulse — Logo Concepts

Three directions, each shown at favicon, app-header, and lockup sizes, on dark and light backgrounds. The recommended concept gets the highest weight in my opinion — but the call is yours.

01

Pulse Dot

wordmark + accent

A clean wordmark with a single green pulse dot, like Stripe's accent or a status indicator. The dot does double-duty: it marks the brand and reads as "alive / monitoring", which fits the product. Most restrained. Best for app headers; works as wordmark-only on the login screen. Favicon falls back to a solid purple square with a green dot.

Aperture

geometric monogram

A solid purple rounded square with a clipped white "P" inside, plus a single green dot at the corner where the P meets the bowl — the dot reads as a status light or the "pulse" peak. Closest to Lexi in spirit (the colon dot is replaced by the corner accent), distinct in shape. Strong at any size.

03

Orbit

abstract glyph

A thin purple ring with a single bright green node sitting on it — a "pulse on the wire" or "satellite orbiting". No letters, fully abstract. Tiny rotation animation makes it feel alive without being literal. Strongest at large sizes (login splash) and as a watermark; needs a wordmark companion in app headers.

Heartbeat

refined EKG with traveling pulse

A cleaner version of the existing pulse-line, but with the spike traveling across the line — the green segment moves left-to-right repeatedly, like a heartbeat monitor in motion. Most literal "pulse" of the set, and reads as motion even at small sizes.

Sonar

center node + expanding rings

A solid green core with two rings rippling outward, alternating green and purple. Reads as "monitoring / signal / alive". Strongest motion of the set; the favicon stays static (just the core dot) so it doesn't distract in tabs. Best at any size, very recognisable as a pulse.

Equalizer

three pulsing bars

Three vertical bars that rise and fall in sequence — like an equaliser, or the bars on a heart-rate monitor. Reads as "performance / metrics / data" without being a chart cliché. Center bar is purple, outer bars green; they pulse staggered so the eye is always pulled across the mark.

07

Dashboard

chart card containing the pulse

A small dashboard tile — faint Y-axis baseline, subtle dotted gridlines, and the EKG pulse drawn as the chart series. A green dot travels along the curve like a real-time monitor. The container is the metaphor (Pulse = a dashboard for QA performance), so the mark literally describes the product. Grid lines drop out at favicon size so it stays legible.

08

Pulse Type

P + ulse drawn as a heartbeat

A typographic wordmark where the P is the fixed anchor and the rest of the word — ulse — is drawn as a heartbeat line. A bright green dot travels along the pulse so the word literally beats. Strong horizontal mark, ideal for sidebar headers and the login screen. The favicon falls back to a solid square with just the P and a green pulse dot, so the brand stays recognisable in browser tabs.

09

Histogram

distribution bars + rolling pulse

Seven bars forming a bell-curve distribution — reads as frequency / sample / measurement, the analyst's icon. A wave of green flashes through the bars left-to-right like a scanline, so you see the pulse "sweep" the dataset. More analytical than the equalizer (concept 6), more chart-like than the dashboard tile (concept 7). Favicon is the static silhouette with the peak bar highlighted; full animation kicks in at app sizes.
